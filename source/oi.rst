Original Intelligence
====================================

For each chapter, a single sentence stating the main point,
written so a child could understand it.

Chapter 1: Before We Begin
---------------------------

*Main point:* Doc has lots of nicknames because he's loved lots of different people in lots of different ways, but they're all the same person writing this book just for you.

Chapter 2: MRIs & a Baby Brother
----------------------------------

*Main point:* Even though Doc grew up in a scary, unloving home, he found real love and peace later, and that same peace can be found by anyone.

Chapter 3: The Rise
---------------------

*Main point:* Growing up means realizing grown-ups don't know everything, which makes big questions bubble up in your mind, and that's a normal, important part of becoming yourself.

Chapter 4: One in Five
------------------------

*Main point:* People misunderstand each other all the time (even best friends do), so instead of getting mad, you should just say "let's start over" and try again.

Chapter 5: QJ
---------------

*Main point:* Since your brain can only really focus on one thing at a time, writing your big, nagging questions down in a journal helps quiet your mind so you can think clearly.

Chapter 6: The Five Questions You've Already Answered
--------------------------------------------------------

*Main point:* Everyone already has answers (even without realizing it) to five huge questions about life, like where we came from and why the world is messy, and those hidden answers quietly steer your whole life.

Chapter 7: Euclid's Five Postulates (the geometry you learned in high school)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

*Main point:* Even math, which seems like the most "provable" thing ever, actually starts by just assuming some things are true without proof, which means math is built on a kind of faith too.

Chapter 8: The Turing Machine (or, why they call me Baba Computer)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

*Main point:* Computers, which seem so logical, actually just trust their basic instructions without ever proving them, showing that even machines run on a kind of faith.

Chapter 9: Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems
---------------------------------------------

*Main point:* A brilliant mathematician proved that no rulebook of math can ever prove everything true or prove itself trustworthy, meaning even math has to stand on something it can't fully explain.

Chapter 10: "No Absolutes", Where That Came From (and What It's Standing On)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

*Main point:* Believing "there are no absolute rights and wrongs" isn't actually neutral; it's itself a guess people take on faith, usually without realizing it.

Chapter 11: Leaning Your Weight
----------------------------------

*Main point:* Trust isn't a blind guess; it's leaning on someone again and again and finding out they hold you up, which is exactly how you learn who (and what) is safe to depend on.

Chapter 12: The Emptied Hand
--------------------------------

*Main point:* When Doc got very sick and nothing he knew could save him, he learned that admitting "I can't do this on my own" and trusting God brought him real peace.

Chapter 13: Knowledge, the Good Gift That Puffs
----------------------------------------------------

*Main point:* Knowing a lot of facts can make you feel proud and important, but real wisdom is using what you know to be kind and helpful, not to feel superior.
